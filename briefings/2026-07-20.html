What Happened Last Week

President Prabowo authorized a comprehensive review of the flagship free nutritious meals program after partner associations threatened to shut down kitchens nationwide over governance disputes.

The Makan Bergizi Gratis program faced a crisis as partner associations threatened to lock kitchen facilities across Indonesia by August 17 if the National Nutrition Agency failed to address what they described as unequal partnership arrangements. The partners, who invested heavily in kitchen infrastructure, complained they bore all operational risks but lacked decision-making power, with suspensions imposed unilaterally when food poisoning incidents occurred. In response, Prabowo permitted the agency to reassess per-portion budgets and recipient targeting, including an option to limit the program to lower income brackets, while watchdog groups argued beneficiaries should be capped at 26 million rather than the planned wider rollout. The government also designated village cooperatives as the sole channel for distributing social assistance and subsidies, tightening control over the program's sprawling logistics network.

The United States and Iran exchanged intensifying strikes for eight consecutive nights after their June ceasefire collapsed over control of the Strait of Hormuz.

Iranian forces struck American military facilities in Oman, Syria, and Jordan on July 17, expanding a conflict that had entered its seventh straight day of U.S. bombing campaigns targeting Iranian radar, bridges, and naval assets. The renewed fighting erupted after Iran attacked ships using an alternative U.S.-managed route through the strait, which Tehran insists it must control and potentially charge fees for under their interpretation of the ceasefire memorandum signed in June. At least 50 people had been killed in Iran since late June when the fragile truce began unraveling, with Iranian officials warning attacks would spread to new areas if U.S. strikes continued. The escalation included two American service members killed in Jordan—the first U.S. military fatalities from Iranian fire since March—prompting Washington to launch retaliatory strikes. Pakistan and other regional powers urged both sides to halt attacks and return to technical-level negotiations, though Iran's foreign ministry said it had no plans to engage with Washington while defending national security interests.

Parliamentary leaders sharply criticized civil servant work culture, prompting the minister to issue a public letter defending millions of government employees.

House Commission II chairman Muhammad Rifqinizamy told the Administrative Reform Minister on July 15 that despite digitalization efforts, the work mentality of civil servants remained unchanged, describing a pattern of clocking in, leaving for coffee, and clocking out again in the evening. Rifqinizamy noted Indonesia ranked 82nd out of 193 countries on the Government Effectiveness Index and 115th of 180 on the Corruption Perception Index, arguing that civil servants lacked the competitiveness expected in the private sector and questioning why they were viewed merely as symbols of job stability. Days after the public rebuke, Minister Rini Widyantini posted handwritten letters to millions of civil servants urging them to keep serving despite fatigue, longing for family, and personal problems, while the National Civil Service Agency head simultaneously conveyed solidarity messages that sparked emotional responses on social media. The exchange highlighted longstanding tensions over bureaucratic performance, with the minister defending achievements in digitalized performance monitoring and expanded competency testing even as legislators pushed for stricter key performance indicators in pending civil service legislation.

Expert Commentary This Week

Writing in Indonesia at Melbourne, Ary Hermawan argues that the dramatic police raid on prosecutor Febrie Adriansyah's residence, which uncovered over $26 million and 74 kilograms of gold, exposes elite fracturing under Prabowo's administration. The seizure represents the latest in a long-running conflict between the National Police and the Attorney General's Office, a battle that predates Prabowo's presidency but has intensified under his rule. Febrie's fall is particularly significant because he led investigations into mega-corruption cases and headed the Forest Area Enforcement Taskforce, a key instrument used by Prabowo to discipline large extractive businesses.

Writing in Fulcrum, Burhanuddin Muhtadi presents survey evidence showing that among Indonesians aware of President Prabowo's cuts to regional transfers, a clear majority opposed the cuts, with 63.3 percent rejecting the policy. The cuts, which reallocate up to $15 billion from regional governments to fund populist national programs, are politically sensitive because they disproportionately affect resource-rich regions outside Java with weak capacity to replace lost revenue. The policy raises democratic concerns by curtailing the fiscal capacity of elected regional governments through unilateral central executive decision, threatening Prabowo's re-election prospects if public dissatisfaction grows.

Writing in The Diplomat, Chris Taylor argues that Indonesia has run out of money to finish building Nusantara, its $32 billion new capital in East Kalimantan, leaving the project in limbo. While the government expected private investors to cover 80 percent of costs, only $4 billion of $13.6 billion in commitments has been realized, and state budget allocations have plummeted 85 percent from 2024 to 2026. The result is a ghost city housing 10,000 people instead of the promised 1.2 million, while Jakarta continues as the actual seat of government and the rupiah has sunk to levels lower than during the 1997-98 Asian financial crisis.
