What Happened Last Week

The Supreme Court on October 30 canceled the high school equivalency certificate used by Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka in his 2024 candidacy.

The court granted the cassation lawsuit filed by advocate Subhan Palal in case number 742 K/TUN/2026, reversing lower court decisions and declaring the certificate invalid and void. Former Coordinating Minister Mahfud MD stated the ruling had retroactive effect, meaning the document is considered never to have existed. On October 6, the Constitutional Court found no convincing evidence that Gibran possessed foreign educational credentials equivalent to a high school diploma. Political parties responded by calling for evaluation of presidential and vice-presidential requirements, though the KPU noted the 2024 election process had concluded. The Supreme Court scheduled a press conference for November 2 to explain the ruling.

Singapore and Indonesia agreed on October 27 to establish a joint task force to address transboundary haze from Indonesian forest fires that reached hazardous levels.

Singapore Foreign Minister Vivian Balakrishnan traveled to Jakarta to meet with Indonesian Foreign Minister Arrmanatha Nasir and President Prabowo after weeks of escalating tensions. Earlier in October, Prime Minister Lawrence Wong warned of legal action under Singapore's Transboundary Haze Pollution Act against Indonesian companies linked to the fires, which generated the worst air pollution Singapore had experienced in over a decade. Indonesia's energy minister had responded by cutting gas exports to Singapore and warning countries not to "lecture" Indonesia. The foreign ministers announced plans to share data on companies linked to the fires and pledged cooperation, with Balakrishnan meeting Prabowo to ease bilateral tensions through quiet diplomacy.

Kopassus designated First Lieutenant AG as a suspect on October 28 in the death of First Lieutenant Jihad Ahmad at a weapons depot four days earlier.

Letda Jihad Ahmad Al-Rasyid died on Saturday, October 24, at the Group 2 Kopassus weapons storage facility in Kartasura, Central Java, after a pistol discharged. Kopassus spokesman Colonel Indra Mardianto explained that Letda AG cocked his pistol without checking the chamber while his finger was on the trigger, causing the weapon to fire. The incident was captured on CCTV and deemed a matter of pure negligence rather than intentional shooting. Kopassus revoked Letda AG's Para, Commando, and Counterterrorism qualifications and detained him at Denpom IV/4 Surakarta for investigation. The suspect faces up to five years in prison under military negligence statutes.

Expert Commentary This Week

Writing in CSIS Indonesia, M. Habib Abiyan Dzakwan argues that Indonesia has failed to fulfill President Prabowo's pledge to maintain good relations with neighboring countries despite his repeated invocations of a thousand friends and zero enemies foreign policy doctrine. Dzakwan contends that over the past two years Indonesia has treated distant partners better than neighbors in both public narrative and actual policies, creating a troubling gap between rhetoric and reality. The piece suggests that turning Prabowo's neighborhood policy promise into concrete action is urgently needed to restore balance to Indonesia's regional relationships.

Sebastian Strangio reports in The Diplomat that Indonesia began reducing natural gas exports to Singapore in late October 2026, diverting supplies to meet domestic electricity needs after a series of major power outages across the archipelago. The move involves redirecting 31 million standard cubic feet per day, approximately 9 percent of Indonesia's total gas exports to Singapore, to power generation in Batam, Bintan, and Sumatra. While Singapore's Energy Market Authority stated the reduction represents less than 2 percent of the country's total daily gas demand and poses no immediate threat to supply, the decision underscores Indonesia's long-standing policy of prioritizing domestic resource needs through its Domestic Market Obligations regime.
