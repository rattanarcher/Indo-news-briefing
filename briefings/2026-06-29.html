What Happened Last Week

Five candidates for Koperasi Desa Merah Putih manager positions died during military-style training between June 17 and 26.

The death toll from latsarmil training reached five participants by Saturday, with victims succumbing to heat stroke, cardiac arrest, tuberculosis complications, and other medical emergencies. The first two deaths were Anisa Muyassaroh and Yonanda Muhammad Taufiq, who died on June 18 and 17 respectively at different training sites. The fifth victim, Nola Dya Sari, died Friday night after complaining of breathing difficulties during a classroom session on agricultural techniques. DPR member Oleh Soleh called on the Ministry of Defense to halt the training, saying participant safety must be prioritized over military-style rigor for civilian cooperative managers. The Defense Ministry announced a comprehensive evaluation with stricter medical oversight and adaptive training methods, while Human Rights Minister Natalius Pigai urged a complete review of the program on Sunday.

A Bung Karno University student leader admitted receiving 20 million rupiah connected to a June 15 demonstration and meeting with Vice President Gibran.

BEM Faculty of Law chair Abdimaludin confessed in a Monday night forum to accepting the money, which he said came through a police officer via a UBK alumnus to prevent protesters from advancing to the Palace area. The admission followed a June 15 demonstration where 15 student representatives were invited to meet Gibran at the Vice Presidential Palace to discuss demands including MBG evaluation and the Kopdes Merah Putih program. Abdimaludin explained the money was distributed among BEM officials and others, with himself keeping 6 million rupiah. PDI-P's Deddy Sitorus called it a "cheap orchestration from above" and demanded Gibran clarify the matter. The Palace said it would investigate, while UBK suspended Abdimaludin and launched an internal ethics probe.

Former President Jokowi launched a political safari in Lampung wearing PSI party regalia, drawing reactions from PDI-P to Gerindra.

Jokowi arrived in Lampung on Friday wearing a PSI-branded shirt and cap sent by his son Kaesang Pangarep, the party's chairman, marking the start of a three-day visit to consolidate the party ahead of the 2029 elections. The former president told a PSI regional coordination meeting his affection for Lampung remained strong and praised local cadres for completing party structures down to the village level. PDI-P politician Guntur Romli characterized the safari as a 2029 campaign to strengthen the Jokowi family's political position, while a UNS professor saw it opening scenarios for "Jokowisme 2.0" that could orbit Gibran or Kaesang outside the Gerindra-PDI-P axis. Gerindra's Prasetyo Hadi congratulated Jokowi, saying the activity showed his health had recovered, while Golkar secretary-general Sarmuji dismissed concerns about voter shifts as normal political competition.

Expert Commentary This Week

Writing for Indonesia at Melbourne, Ian Wilson analyzes how viral crime panics become pretext for expanding military visibility in Jakarta's streets. A spate of motorcycle robberies sparked online panic fueled by artificially amplified and fabricated TikTok posts, including wholly staged incidents, prompting government responses despite evidence that reported incidents actually declined from February to May. TNI Chief Maruli Simanjuntak proposed joint military-police patrols as deterrence through visibility, which Wilson suggests amounts to mission creep that aligns with the Prabowo administration's massive military expansion through development infantry battalions and enlarged territorial commands.

Writing for Indonesia at Melbourne, Aristyo Darmawan examines great power tensions brewing in Indonesia's North Natuna Sea as Russia's Zarubezhneft assumes full control of the strategic Tuna block oil field. The block, located just 14 kilometers from Vietnam in Indonesia's exclusive economic zone, overlaps with China's illegal nine-dash line and has faced persistent Chinese pressure including 2021 protests and coast guard maneuvers near drilling rigs. Prabowo's unprecedented joint statement with Xi Jinping recognizing overlapping claims and promoting joint development creates uncertainty about how this affects Russian-led oil and gas exploitation in territory China considers its own.

Writing in The Diplomat, James Guild investigates the collapse of Indonesia's booming tech sector from a 2021 peak of over $9 billion in venture capital to just $300 million in 2025. Beyond rising interest rates ending easy money, Guild identifies governance failures including the eFishery fraud where founders fabricated revenue numbers, and struggles by legitimate firms like GoTo to create shareholder value, with its market cap plummeting from $30 billion to $3 billion since 2022. While these platforms reduce transaction costs and boost spending in the Indonesian economy, they have failed to achieve consistent profitability, undermining investor confidence in the sector's regulatory oversight and long-term viability.
