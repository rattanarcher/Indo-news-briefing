What Happened Last Week

Indonesia overhauled its flagship Free Nutritious Meals program mid-week amid corruption allegations, business operator backlash, and mounting student demonstrations.

Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka committed to strengthening oversight of the MBG program on Wednesday after revelations that former National Nutrition Agency officials had been detained in a graft probe. The government announced a moratorium on new kitchens and introduced a grading system for existing service points, while suspending the program during school holidays to conduct a comprehensive evaluation. Officials projected that efficiency measures could save approximately Rp 1 trillion monthly, with the 2026 budget already slashed from Rp 335 trillion to Rp 268 trillion. The reforms drew fierce resistance: business operators who had invested in MBG kitchens staged protests Friday, while thousands of students from Trisakti and other universities converged on the DPR building demanding transparency on the program alongside fuel price relief. By week's end, DPR leaders promised to bring student concerns to the government and claimed the reforms would save Rp 70 trillion annually, though critics questioned whether efficiency or political pressure was driving the sudden pivot.

The United States and Iran signed a preliminary peace deal on June 15 to end their three-month war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, though implementation quickly became contentious.

The 14-point memorandum of understanding committed both sides to a 60-day ceasefire extension, the lifting of naval blockades, and toll-free passage through Hormuz during negotiations on Iran's nuclear program and sanctions relief. President Trump declared the deal "complete" and global markets rallied on the news, with oil prices dropping nearly 5 percent. But strains surfaced almost immediately: Iran threatened to close the Strait again by Sunday over continued Israeli strikes in Lebanon, accusing Washington of failing to enforce the ceasefire. A planned signing ceremony in Switzerland was abruptly postponed Friday with Vice President JD Vance's trip delayed, while Iranian officials traveled to Geneva demanding U.S. compliance with the agreement. The framework faced domestic criticism in both countries, with U.S. lawmakers questioning whether Iran had gained too many concessions and Iranian hardliners skeptical that sanctions would truly be lifted, leaving the prospect of a permanent settlement uncertain as the week closed.

Coalition parties pressed PDI-P throughout the week to clarify whether it supports or opposes the Prabowo government, drawing sharp rebukes from the former ruling party.

PKB publicly demanded Friday that PDI-P choose between coalition membership and opposition rather than remaining in a "gray area," while Golkar legislators similarly questioned the party's ambiguous stance. PDI-P officials fired back over the weekend, with a spokesperson telling Golkar to focus on power outages instead of PDI-P's positioning. The party reiterated that it operates as a "balancing force" outside the cabinet but willing to support policies aligned with Pancasila values, a position it formalized at its January national congress. A PDI-P legislator disclosed Sunday that fellow lawmakers from coalition parties had privately complained they could not freely criticize the government, while PDI-P officials insisted that countries where all parties join the government are authoritarian. The rhetorical clash underscored ongoing tensions between the Megawati-led party and the Prabowo administration it declined to join, even as speculation persisted about whether PDI-P might eventually enter a formal coalition.

Expert Commentary This Week

East Asia Forum's commentary argues that Indonesia faces a looming crisis of economic confidence as President Prabowo Subianto's statist interventionism tests investor trust. The piece warns that growing pressure on the rupiah, public finances, and foreign investment is exposing the risks of resource nationalism and cavalier policymaking, noting that Prabowo's export brokerage scheme was enacted with little meaningful consultation across government and industry. Unless confidence in economic management improves, efforts to strengthen sovereignty may ultimately undermine the growth and credibility needed to sustain it.

Writing in Fulcrum, Kevin Zongzhe Li argues that Indonesia has become a nickel state in formation, leveraging mineral rents the way petrostates leverage hydrocarbons, with nickel contributing heavily to GDP and exports. While Jokowi's downstreaming forced processing onshore, technology transfer remained limited with Chinese firms controlling at least 75 percent of refining capacity. Under Prabowo, the administration has tightened state control through export proceeds rules, higher royalties, quota cuts, and environmental enforcement to extract more value from the industry without yet deepening domestic technological capacity.

Writing in Fulcrum, Fannesa Adisty Laksmita argues that deepening political constraints and narrowing civil society space are stalling the Indonesian Armed Forces' already limited reform agenda since the Reformasi era. The 2025 TNI Law revision drew public criticism for backsliding on earlier progress by providing legal justification for expanded military roles under loosely defined military operations other than war. Based on interviews with civil society organizations, Laksmita identifies that military reform remains an elite-driven agenda resulting from negotiated rather than consolidated democratic transition, directly undermining national defense provision and eroding civilian control. Writing in The Diplomat, Sebastian Strangio reports that MSCI has lowered Indonesia's information flow criterion to negative, citing structural issues in the opacity in shareholding structures and concerns about coordinated trading that undermine price formation. The decision comes just a week before MSCI announces whether to downgrade Indonesia to frontier market status, a move that could trigger billions in capital outflows after the stock market has already fallen 29 percent in 2026. The transparency concerns dovetail with broader worries about Prabowo's economic management, including high-spending populist policies and increased state intervention that have unsettled investors and undermined Indonesia's reputation for fiscal conservatism.
