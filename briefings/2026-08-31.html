What Happened Last Week

DPR leadership pledged to pass the Asset Forfeiture Bill by December 15 after demonstrations on August 27 brought anti-corruption demands to parliament.

Multiple groups converged on the DPR building on Wednesday, August 27, with the Pati United Community Alliance (AMPB) demanding passage of the long-stalled Asset Forfeiture Bill, which has lingered in legislative limbo since 2008. Police had blocked donation accounts and investigated organizers in the days before the demonstration, though Law Minister Yusril denied ordering the account freeze. During an audience with protesters, Deputy Speaker Sufmi Dasco Ahmad signed a commitment letter pledging to finalize the bill by December 15, 2026, and stated he would resign if the deadline was missed. The commitment drew attention when DPR Speaker Puan Maharani's signature was notably absent from the letter signed by four other parliamentary leaders. PDI-P Secretary General Hasto Kristiyanto defended the absence by stating that DPR leadership operates on a collective-collegial basis and insisted the party remained committed to passing the anti-corruption legislation.

Indonesia's first aircraft carrier, the former Italian vessel Giuseppe Garibaldi, departed from Taranto on August 24 bound for Indonesia where it will be commissioned as KRI Gajah Mada.

The carrier departed Italy on Monday night under a mixed Italian-Indonesian crew, still flying the Italian flag and escorted by Italian frigates for the more than 20-day voyage through the Suez Canal and Red Sea. Navy Chief of Staff Admiral Muhammad Ali confirmed the ship is scheduled to arrive between September 15 and 20, with a formal commissioning ceremony planned for September 25 that President Prabowo Subianto is expected to attend. Italy transferred the 14,150-tonne vessel free of charge to eliminate maintenance costs, while Indonesia budgeted between €350 million and €818 million for reactivation and modernization. The carrier will be renamed after Gajah Mada, the 14th-century Majapahit military commander, and is scheduled to debut at the TNI's 81st anniversary sailing pass in October. A new aircraft carrier base in Lampung is in its finishing phase to accommodate the vessel, making Indonesia the second Southeast Asian nation after Thailand to operate an aircraft carrier.

President Prabowo visited earthquake-stricken East Nusa Tenggara on August 26 and asked survivors whether to continue the Free Nutritious Meals program.

Prabowo traveled to Nagekeo Regency eleven days after a magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck the region on August 15, deliberately delaying his visit to avoid disrupting evacuation efforts. During a coordination meeting, the president distributed IDR 200 billion in aid to the provincial government and eight affected regencies on Flores Island, while BNPB reported 105 deaths, 1,678 injuries, and 179,037 displaced. At an evacuation center, Prabowo received reports from local officials about the benefits of the Makan Bergizi Gratis (MBG) program and directly asked earthquake survivors whether to continue the initiative, then pledged to maintain the program to ensure health for children, pregnant women, and the elderly. The president also highlighted how 71 MBG kitchens were deployed to help feed evacuees in the disaster zone.

Expert Commentary This Week

Writing in Fulcrum, former Indonesian Ambassador to the WTO Iman Pambagyo argues that the Indonesia-US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade, while offering tangible market access benefits, could constrain its future policy space in areas like industrial policy and digital regulation. Pambagyo notes the agreement is asymmetrical, with Indonesia appearing to have undertaken broader commitments than the United States, particularly on non-tariff measures and regulatory disciplines. He contends the agreement should not be viewed merely through tariff preferences but raises fundamental questions about policy flexibility, regulatory autonomy, and Indonesia's long-term development strategy, deserving careful scrutiny before ratification.

Writing in Fulcrum, Luna Ge Lai and Maria Monica Wihardja argue that China's restrained response to the Indonesia-US trade agreement masks serious concerns about provisions that could indirectly target Chinese interests. The authors identify four key concerns among Chinese experts, including export control provisions that pressure Indonesia to restrict transactions with firms on US blacklists like Huawei and ZTE, and a poison pill clause allowing Washington to terminate the agreement if Indonesia concludes a free trade deal with a country deemed harmful to US interests. They contend that Beijing's restraint reflects the high stakes involved and should not be read as acquiescence, as China retains various remedies in its toolbox if it chooses to respond.

Writing in The Diplomat, Febry Triantama argues that despite China's dominant economic position in Indonesia and favorable public opinion, China is far behind in defense sector influence compared to the United States. Triantama notes that during 2003-2022, Indonesia conducted only four joint military exercises with China versus 110 with the United States, and Indonesia has adopted almost no Chinese weapons systems throughout its history. The author contends the August 2026 2+2 dialogue, which produced agreements on increased joint military exercises and plans for joint munition factories, represents China's effort to close this gap and secure defense influence that matches its economic footprint in Indonesia.
