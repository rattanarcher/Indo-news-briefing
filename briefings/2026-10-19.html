What Happened Last Week

The Constitutional Court found no proof of Vice President Gibran's high school diploma but declined to disqualify him, leaving the matter to parliament.

On October 6, the Constitutional Court ruled that petitioners lacked standing to challenge Gibran Rakabuming Raka's educational qualifications, but in its reasoning the court stated it found no convincing evidence of a high school-equivalent diploma for the vice president. The decision triggered calls from PDIP politicians for the House of Representatives to exercise its constitutional authority through an inquiry or impeachment motion. By mid-week, Commission II announced it was studying the ruling and would consider the matter during deliberations on electoral law reform. On Friday, former President Jokowi dismissed the controversy as a political operation designed to damage his and Gibran's reputations, claiming "free riders" had exploited the issue. By Sunday, analysts noted the House faced a constitutional dilemma: the ball now sits with the legislature, which could pursue impeachment but appears reluctant given the political will required.

President Prabowo conducted three major reshuffles in October, culminating in a swap between army chief Maruli Simanjuntak and deputy TNI commander Tandyo Budi Revita.

After reshuffling 16 cabinet positions on October 1—including elevating Arrmanatha Nasir to foreign minister and Sugiono to coordinating minister—Prabowo carried out a second round on October 2 appointing 26 presidential advisers. On October 15, he executed his third reshuffle of the month, swapping the positions of General Maruli Simanjuntak and General Tandyo Budi Revita: Maruli moved from army chief to deputy TNI commander while Tandyo took over as head of the army. The formal handover ceremony took place the following day at TNI headquarters. Observers questioned what Prabowo was pursuing with three rounds of appointments in two weeks as the administration neared its two-year mark. On Saturday evening, the president summoned his full cabinet to Bogor Palace—not for a policy meeting but to host a banquet for visiting Russian Deputy Prime Minister Denis Manturov, introducing his ministerial team to the Russian delegation.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres arrived in Indonesia for a three-day visit to strengthen multilateral cooperation and honor Indonesian peacekeepers.

Guterres landed on Saturday, October 17, for what marks the first visit by a UN chief during Prabowo Subianto's presidency. His itinerary included meetings to discuss cooperation within the multilateral system and a planned visit to the TNI Peacekeeping Mission Center in Bogor, where he would lay a wreath honoring 50 fallen Indonesian peacekeepers and meet troops deploying to the Central African Republic. On Sunday, Prabowo received Guterres at his Hambalang residence, where the secretary-general praised Indonesia's foreign policy leadership and support for Palestine. The visit holds particular significance: Guterres is set to step down in December 2026, making this among his final official trips. He was scheduled to deliver a speech titled "Eight Decades of the United Nations: What Comes Next?" at the Foreign Ministry on Monday before returning to New York.
